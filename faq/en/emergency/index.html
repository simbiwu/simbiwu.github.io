Emergency screen privacy

Cover current content quickly when a supervisor, family member, or stranger approaches, or before presenting and sharing a screen.

12 questions · Last reviewed 2026-08-06

How can I quickly protect my screen when someone walks up to my PC?

Use the fastest reliable action with the least impact: hide windows or lock Windows when you are at the keyboard; rely on a preconfigured device or camera trigger only after you have stepped away.

How can I protect privacy when I cannot reach the keyboard or mouse?

You need a trigger that was enabled and tested in advance, such as removing a USB device, moving a Bluetooth device out of range, or using a camera gesture. Without prior setup, software cannot infer your intent in the moment.

Read the full answer →

In an urgent moment, should I lock Windows or only hide windows?

Hide windows for brief visual cover. Lock Windows whenever someone might continue using the PC. Neither action replaces encryption for sensitive files.

How can I protect privacy without losing unsaved work?

Use window hiding or Windows lock as the first layer, and leave force-close, restart, and shutdown actions disabled. Important documents should still rely on the application's save and recovery features.

How can I protect office privacy when a manager suddenly walks over?

Choose window hiding or Windows lock according to the sensitivity and avoid force-closing applications in a panic. SuperWatchDog does not decide what you are protecting; you choose the trigger and actions in advance.

What if I have no time to organize private windows before screen sharing?

When possible, share one application and disable notifications. If an ad-hoc presentation has already started, use a configured action to hide windows that should not be shown. This is a fallback, not a replacement for a pre-meeting check.

How can I stop private content being seen at home, in a dorm, or in public?

Start with screen placement, hidden notification previews, and Windows lock, then prepare one familiar quick trigger. In shared environments, do not rely only on closing a window; lock the PC whenever you step away.

What should I do if someone approaches while I am gaming or viewing private content?

Use hiding or Windows lock without terminating applications, rather than force-closing a game, player, or document. Users decide what to protect; the software does not evaluate the type of content.

What is the difference between Win+L and SuperWatchDog?

Win+L is the direct built-in shortcut for locking Windows and works well when you can reach the keyboard. SuperWatchDog is for users who want other trigger methods and their own combination of window, lock-screen, and vault actions.

Read the full answer →

Why do Alt+Tab or closing a window not solve every privacy emergency?

Switching or closing can work for one window when your hands are free. It is less dependable with many windows, full-screen shortcuts, unsaved files, or a person who may continue using the PC.

Read the full answer →

What is the difference between a boss key and SuperWatchDog?

A traditional boss key usually hides and restores selected windows with a keystroke. SuperWatchDog offers more trigger choices and lets users combine window handling, Windows lock, and vault protection. Neither judges content or overrides workplace rules.

Read the full answer →

What is the difference between file encryption and emergency privacy protection?

File encryption mainly protects stored data while it is locked. Emergency privacy protection handles the current screen, signed-in session, and open windows. They complement each other; neither automatically replaces the other.
